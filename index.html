STAGE 1: Thermal Pre-Treatment & Fluorine Scission T = 450°C - 500°C | [Ref: Kang et al. 2010]
[-CH₂-CF₂-]ₙ (PVDF Binder) ➔ 2n HF(g) + n C(s)
2 HF(g) + Ca(OH)₂ (Hydrated Lime) ➔ CaF₂ ↓ + 2 H₂O

Stoichiometric Basis: Decomposing 1.0 kg of PVDF binder evolves 0.625 kg of corrosive HF gas. Neutralization requires 1.157 kg of Ca(OH)2, yielding 1.218 kg of stable CaF2 filter cake. The remaining graphite and mixed metal oxides are then separated via physical froth flotation before chemical leaching.

STAGE 2: Reductive Sulfuric Acid Leaching (NMC / LFP) pH 0.5 - 1.2 | T = 60°C | [Ref: Meshram et al. 2018]
2 LiMeO₂ + 3 H₂SO₄ + H₂O₂ ➔ Li₂SO₄(aq) + 2 MeSO₄(aq) + 4 H₂O + O₂(g) ↑

*(Where Me = Ni, Co, Mn)*. Stoichiometric Basis: Dissolving 1.0 kg of LiNi0.6Mn0.2Co0.2O2 requires 1.51 kg H2SO4 (100% basis) and 0.175 kg H2O2 (100% basis). In practice, a 1.35x excess is maintained to prevent metal re-precipitation.

STAGE 3: Oxidative Hydrolysis & Goethite Impurity Removal pH 3.5 - 5.0 | T = 90°C | [Ref: Zhang et al. 2020]
Fe³⁺ + 2 H₂O + NaOH ➔ FeO(OH) ↓ (Goethite) + Na⁺ + 2 H⁺
Al³⁺ + 3 NaOH ➔ Al(OH)₃ ↓ + 3 Na⁺

Stoichiometric Basis: Removing 1.0 kg of dissolved iron as Goethite consumes 0.716 kg NaOH and yields 1.591 kg dry FeO(OH) sludge while releasing acid into solution.

STAGE 4: Solvent Extraction Saponification & Carbonate Precipitation pH 6.5 - 9.5 | [Ref: Swain 2017]
H₂A₂ (Cyanex 272) + 2 NaOH ➔ 2 NaA (Saponified Extractant) + 2 H₂O
Li₂SO₄(aq) + Na₂CO₃(aq) ➔ Li₂CO₃ ↓ (Lithium Carbonate) + Na₂SO₄(aq)

Stoichiometric Basis: Precipitating 1.0 kg of battery-grade Li2CO3 requires 1.434 kg of Na2CO3 and produces 1.921 kg of Na2SO4 in solution, which is subsequently crystallized in the Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD) circuit.

2. Molar Moles Conversion Step Inputs ➔ Moles

Elemental mass (m) is derived from the feedstock composition fractions. Molar quantities (n) are calculated as: n_i (kmol) = [ Feedstock (MT) × Mass Fraction w_i × 1,000 ] / Molar Mass (kg/kmol)

3. Acid Dissolution Stoichiometry H2SO4 Reagent

Theoretical sulfuric acid required (100% basis): n_H2SO4,theo = 0.5×n_Li + 1.0×n_Ni + 1.0×n_Co + 1.0×n_Mn + 1.5×n_Fe Accounting for industrial 98% concentration & excess factor (Fexcess): Mass H2SO4 (98%) = (n_H2SO4,theo × 98.079 / 1,000) × F_excess / 0.98

4. Reductant & Alkali Neutralization H2O2 & NaOH

Reductive dissolution of trivalent cobalt (Co3+ → Co2+) and quadrivalent manganese (Mn4+ → Mn2+): n_H2O2 = 0.5×n_Co + 0.5×n_Mn Alkali consumed for Iron Goethite precipitation & neutralization: n_NaOH = 3.0×n_Fe + 2.0×n_UnreactedAcid

5. Battery Salt Crystallization Yields Hydrated Precursors

Hydrated salt masses are calculated by combining metal kmoles dissolved (yield η) with hydrate molecular weights: MW(NiSO4·6H2O) = 262.85 g/mol | MW(CoSO4·7H2O) = 281.10 g/mol MW(MnSO4·H2O) = 169.02 g/mol | MW(Li2CO3) = 73.89 g/mol

📄 Simulation Engine Summary Documentation

1. Purpose

This simulator provides a quantitative chemical and resource accounting model for refining spent lithium-ion battery "black mass." It dynamically computes mass conservation vectors, required acid/alkali reagent flows, and precise output yields of battery-grade precursor salts based on varying feedstock chemistries (NMC, LFP).

2. Methodology

The engine leverages a rigid stoichiometric mass-balance framework rather than arbitrary constants. It transforms user-selected chemistry profiles into elemental molar weights, applying verified chemical equations for reductive leaching, impurity precipitation (e.g., Goethite removal), and solvent extraction. Reagent excesses and water recycling ratios are fully adjustable to model different plant efficiencies.

3. Key Findings & Insights

  • Reagent Hotspots: Sulfuric acid and Sodium Hydroxide constitute the vast majority (~86%) of total chemical operational mass, highly sensitive to transition metal content.
  • Water Footprint: Implementing Mechanical Vapor Recompression (MVR) for a Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD) loop dramatically reduces fresh water drawdown (e.g., saving over 4,000 m³ per 1,000 MT batch at an 85% loop).
  • Yield Distribution: High-nickel chemistries (like NMC 811) shift the primary mass output heavily towards Nickel Sulfate and resultant Sodium Sulfate by-products.

4. Regulatory & Literature Basis

All recovery calculations and benchmarks align with the MoEFCC Battery Waste Management Rules (2022) and CPCB Zero Liquid Discharge mandates. Kinetic parameters and stoichiometric requirements are drawn directly from peer-reviewed literature, including Meshram et al. (2018) for leaching dynamics and the NITI Aayog Critical Minerals Strategy for precise cathode bill-of-materials (BOM).
